## Scoped OUT — the conversational interface

Alex's strongest ask, 16:09: *"Everything that is static, where they cannot speak with a
chatbot has no value."* His current workaround is screenshotting DealForge into ChatGPT
and pasting the result back.

**Decision (Lloyd, 2026-09-04): not building this into DealForge.** It's a separate product;
folding it in turns every tab into a chat surface and nothing ships. V2 is UI-level changes
plus the case-study library, all of it programmatic.

## Cross-cutting — case studies on four tabs

Ryan's design (1:06:27), accepted by Alex: the same control on every tab, switching between
the prospect's data and a real client's. *"This is your lead list. This is what Cedric's
looked like."*

Alex's matching rule (1:07:33): three case studies per deal — one similar by **TAM**, one by
**offering**, one by **business model**. Auto-selected, manually overridable (1:03:37).

Agreed display treatment: **swap** the panel on Lead List / Calendar Invite / Webinar
Experience; **side by side** on ROI, because the point there is comparison (1:04:07:
*"I see two or three in like the same tab just showing up"*).

## Per tab

**Lead List** — three client names with similar TAM plus the results they got (1:05:11).

**Calendar Invite** — the headline matters most because it doubles as the presentation
headline (44:21). Observed failure with Edwin (42:38): the AI wrote the invite as if QS were
the client, *"mixing our offer with his offer."* Two new sub-tabs requested (59:24): a
screenshot of the automation/infra tooling *(name garbled in transcript as "Scarpi, Hermes"
— needs confirming)*, and a VA section. Alex's VA script (59:53): *"You don't have to run any
of this. It's run by the VAs, five hours a week. They come from us. They are pre-trained."*

**Webinar Experience** — an AI-built slide deck following a fixed 9-beat storyline (47:05):
bold claim → audience → problem/cost of not changing → dream state → case study before/after →
walk from booking to result → how the offer removes risk (investment, failure, communication
gap, time) → solution offering → three CTAs. Also links to real client webinar recordings.

**ROI Model** — two passes. Pass 1: maximum scenario, TAM ÷ 4 = monthly TAM, most conservative
inputs available (48:49: *"I usually take whatever they gave me and I even downgraded by
another 5%"*). Pass 2: reverse-engineer invite volume from their goal. Worked example (54:08):
40,000 invites = $670/mo, plus $2,400/mo service = ~$3,000/mo to win 3 clients → stated as 30x
ROI. Also flagged (35:02): the model ignores sales-cycle revenue delay, which *"decreases the
credibility of the ROI."*

**Onboarding** — case-study storytelling only, using the existing 10-min client interview.
Alex explicitly wants nothing else here (1:04:47).

**Due Diligence** — links to real client webinar recordings (1:01:09). Lloyd flagged those
links may not exist for every client.

**Case Studies tab** — Alex: *"we can kick out this thing... no value"* (1:02:02), then
softened when pushed back on. **Unresolved.**
